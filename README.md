# Free Will & Human Decision-Making Analytics

> An interdisciplinary data analytics project exploring human decision-making, perceived agency, behavioral constraints, and philosophical perspectives on free will using statistics and machine learning.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

**Free Will & Human Decision-Making Analytics** explores the relationship between human decision-making behavior and philosophical concepts surrounding **free will, personal agency, determinism, choice, and responsibility**.

The project combines:

- Behavioral Analytics
- Psychology
- Philosophy
- Data Analytics
- Statistics
- Machine Learning
- Decision Science

A synthetic dataset containing **1,000 simulated decision cases** is analyzed across different decision-making scenarios including career, financial, relationship, health, education, and consumer decisions.

The purpose is not to prove whether free will objectively exists. Instead, the project demonstrates how concepts such as **perceived agency, self-control, cognitive reflection, stress, emotional intensity, social influence, and decision confidence** can be structured and explored through data analytics.

---

# 🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

1. Analyze factors associated with human decision-making.
2. Study perceived agency and decision autonomy.
3. Examine internal and external constraints on decision-making.
4. Investigate relationships between stress, reflection, social influence, and confidence.
5. Build analytical decision-making indices.
6. Apply statistical analysis and machine learning.
7. Identify recurring behavioral decision patterns.
8. Connect behavioral observations with philosophical perspectives on free will.
9. Demonstrate responsible interpretation of psychologically themed data.

# 🧠 Philosophical Perspectives

The project uses philosophy as an interpretive framework rather than attempting to mathematically prove a philosophical position.

## 1. Libertarian Free Will

The libertarian perspective generally emphasizes genuine freedom of choice and the possibility of meaningful alternative actions.

From an analytical perspective, this project examines variables such as:

- Perceived agency
- Self-control
- Decision autonomy
- Cognitive reflection

---

## 2. Hard Determinism

Hard determinism emphasizes causal factors influencing human decisions and questions whether decisions can ultimately be considered freely chosen.

The project therefore examines potential constraints such as:

- Stress
- Social influence
- Time pressure
- Emotional intensity
- Information availability

---

## 3. Compatibilism

Compatibilism explores the possibility that meaningful freedom can coexist with causal influences when decisions reflect an individual's internal reasons, preferences, and values.

This perspective is particularly relevant to variables such as:

- Perceived agency
- Self-control
- Decision confidence
- Cognitive reflection
- Decision satisfaction

---

## 4. Philosophical Uncertainty

Behavioral data cannot independently resolve the philosophical question of whether humans possess metaphysical free will.

Therefore, the project does **not** claim to prove:

> "Humans have free will"

or

> "Humans do not have free will."

Instead, it demonstrates how observable decision-making factors can be analyzed alongside philosophical interpretations.

# 📊 Dataset

The project contains **1,000 simulated decision cases**.

The dataset is completely synthetic and does not represent real individuals.

## Decision Scenarios

The simulated cases cover:

- Career Decisions
- Financial Decisions
- Relationship Decisions
- Health Decisions
- Education Decisions
- Consumer Decisions
